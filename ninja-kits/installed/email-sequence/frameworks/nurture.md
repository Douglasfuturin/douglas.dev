# Nurture Sequence Framework

> **Goal:** keep [VOICE OWNER] top-of-mind between welcome and offer. Build the relationship deep enough that when the offer drops, the reader is already half-sold.

Nurture is where most lists die — operators ship welcome + launch but leave a multi-week gap of nothing. The reader cools off, archives, and unsubscribes. Nurture is the connective tissue that turns one-time openers into long-term readers.

## Default arc — 6 emails over 14 days

| # | Day after welcome ends | Send time | Beat | Body purpose | Length |
|---|-----------------------|-----------|------|--------------|--------|
| 1 | 2 | 9am local | **Story → lesson** | a specific moment that teaches one idea | 350-550 words |
| 2 | 4 | 9am local | **Tactical teach** | one thing the reader can do today — concrete, immediate | 300-450 words |
| 3 | 6 | 9am local | **Reframe** | challenge a belief the reader probably holds | 300-500 words |
| 4 | 9 | 9am local | **Case study** | a different story / different angle than the welcome proof | 350-500 words |
| 5 | 11 | 9am local | **Behind-the-scenes** | something raw — what's been going on lately, what [VOICE OWNER] is working on | 250-400 words |
| 6 | 14 | 9am local | **Soft offer** | the offer, framed as "I built this because..." | 300-450 words |

## Two valid styles

**Andre Chaperon / Autoresponder Madness style — narrative arc.**
Each email opens a loop and closes one from earlier. The 6 emails feel like 6 chapters of one story. Best for: high-ticket, considered purchases, info products. Worst for: e-commerce SKUs.

**Ben Settle / daily email style — standalone gems.**
Each email is a complete thought. No multi-email narrative. Every email teaches one idea + soft pitches. Best for: list-building businesses where the reader stays on the list forever. Worst for: short funnels with a fixed offer.

Pick one style per nurture sequence. Don't mix mid-sequence — the rhythm change is jarring.

## Psychology of each beat

**Story → lesson:** stories rebuild the bond after the welcome sequence's polish. Get specific — a real moment, real dialogue, real stakes. The lesson should emerge from the story, not be tacked on at the end.

**Tactical teach:** the reader's lizard brain asks "what's in it for me right now?" One concrete tip earns the next 4 emails. Don't gate it. Give it away in the email body.

**Reframe:** the reader has beliefs about [INDUSTRY] that are limiting them. Pick one common belief and dismantle it. This positions [VOICE OWNER] as someone who sees what others miss.

**Case study:** different from welcome's proof — different person, different angle, different metric. Variety prevents "okay I get it, you've helped one person" syndrome.

**Behind-the-scenes:** humanizes [VOICE OWNER]. What they're building, what's broken, what they're frustrated about. Raw beats polished. This is the email that converts a reader from "I follow this guy" to "I'm rooting for this guy."

**Soft offer:** the offer presented as the natural conclusion of the last 5 emails. Not "BUY NOW" — but "if any of this resonated, this is where we go deeper."

## Rules

- **Pacing matters.** 14 days, not 7 — readers need recovery time between emails. Daily emails work for a separate (opt-in) cadence, not for the nurture sequence.
- **Cliffhang strategically.** Open at least one loop in email 1 that doesn't close until email 4 or 5. Loops drive re-opens.
- **Don't repeat welcome material.** Reader just read it. New stories, new proof, new angles.
- **No urgency.** Same rule as welcome — save it for launches.
- **Soft pitch in every email is okay.** A one-line "this is what I do" at the bottom of every email teaches the reader you have an offer, without selling on every send.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Why it kills the sequence |
|---------|---------------------------|
| 6 standalone "tips" emails | No emotional connection forms — reader treats it as a newsletter |
| Story → no lesson | Reader bonds with [VOICE OWNER] but doesn't learn anything they can use |
| Reframe email is contrarian for the sake of being contrarian | If the new belief doesn't help the reader, it just feels edgy |
| Behind-the-scenes feels staged | Reader can sense polished raw vs. real raw |
| Soft offer in email 6 is suddenly hard sell | The arc was relaxed, then the close is panicked — jarring |
