# Welcome Sequence Framework

> **Goal:** turn a stranger who just opted in into someone who knows [VOICE OWNER], trusts the work, and is primed to read every future email.

The welcome is the most-opened sequence in the entire AIOS — most lists see 60-80% open rates on email 1 and 30-50% by email 5. This is the highest-leverage 5 emails [CLIENT NAME] will ever send. Spend the time.

## Default arc — 5 emails over 7 days

| # | Day | Send time | Beat | Body purpose | Length | CTA strength |
|---|-----|-----------|------|--------------|--------|--------------|
| 1 | 0 | within 5 min of opt-in | **Confirm + identity** | deliver lead magnet, set sender expectation, establish voice | 80-150 words | soft (read this / hit reply) |
| 2 | 1 | 9am local | **Origin story** | who [VOICE OWNER] is and why this work matters | 250-400 words | soft (reply with your story) |
| 3 | 3 | 9am local | **Big idea** | the unique mechanism / contrarian belief that defines the work | 300-500 words | medium (read the proof) |
| 4 | 5 | 9am local | **Proof** | one specific case study or transformation — concrete numbers | 250-400 words | medium (link to deeper case study) |
| 5 | 7 | 9am local | **Soft pitch** | what's next — the offer, framed as logical next step (not pressure) | 200-350 words | hard (book / buy / apply) — but no urgency |

## Psychology behind the arc

**Email 1 — Confirm + identity:**
The reader expects a delivery email. Give them that, but make it sound like a human wrote it — because the first 30 seconds set the tone for every future email. Lead with the deliverable, then drop one line of voice that tells them what they signed up for.

**Email 2 — Origin story:**
Bond before sell. The reader doesn't know [VOICE OWNER] yet. A specific moment — not a polished bio — earns the next click. The story should connect to the same pain the lead magnet addressed.

**Email 3 — Big idea:**
This is the most important email in the sequence. State the contrarian belief that explains why [VOICE OWNER]'s work is different from the obvious alternatives in [INDUSTRY]. If the reader nods at this email, every future email gets read.

**Email 4 — Proof:**
After the big idea lands, the reader's question is "okay, but does it actually work?" One specific, concrete proof beats five vague ones. Real names, real numbers, real before/after — never "one of my clients."

**Email 5 — Soft pitch:**
By now they know you, trust the angle, and have seen it work. The ask should feel inevitable, not pushy. No fake urgency on a welcome sequence — these are warm, not hot.

## Rules

- **Email 1 is the trust gate.** If this one is generic, the rest of the list gets ignored. Read like the start of a story, not the start of a funnel.
- **No personalization beyond first name in email 1.** Other personalization feels invasive when the relationship is 5 minutes old.
- **One CTA per email.** Stacked CTAs split attention and tank the click.
- **Build the loop in email 2, close it in email 5.** A narrative thread across the 5 emails dramatically improves email-5 open rate.
- **Mention the lead magnet in email 1 only.** After that, the reader has it. Don't keep re-selling what they already received.
- **No urgency.** Welcome sequences are about trust, not pressure. Save scarcity for launches.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Why it kills the sequence |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Email 1 is automated-looking | Reader assumes every future email will be the same, archives the rest |
| Origin story is too polished | Feels like a sales page, not a person — bond doesn't form |
| Big idea is vague ("be authentic", "work smarter") | No mechanism = no reason to keep reading |
| Proof email lists 5 wins instead of telling 1 story | Stats numb; stories stick |
| Email 5 stacks CTAs (reply OR book OR buy) | Cognitive load → no action |
| Fake urgency added to email 5 ("48 hours only!") | Reader has no relationship yet, this kills trust |
