# Evergreen Sequence Framework

> **Goal:** convert tripwire / low-ticket buyers (or warm leads) into core-offer buyers over 30 days. Replaces launches for businesses that need consistent revenue, not spikes.

The evergreen sequence is the workhorse of subscription / membership / SaaS-adjacent businesses. Instead of running a launch every 90 days, the offer is always open — and the email sequence walks each new buyer toward it on their own clock.

## Default arc — 10 emails over 30 days

| # | Day | Beat | Body purpose | Length |
|---|-----|------|--------------|--------|
| 1 | 0 | **Identity** | who [VOICE OWNER] is, why this email series exists | 250-400 words |
| 2 | 3 | **Big idea** | the contrarian belief that defines the work | 350-500 words |
| 3 | 5 | **Tactical teach #1** | something the reader can do today | 300-450 words |
| 4 | 8 | **Story → lesson** | a moment that proves the big idea | 400-550 words |
| 5 | 11 | **Tactical teach #2** | another concrete tip — different angle | 300-450 words |
| 6 | 14 | **Case study** | a transformation story with real numbers | 400-600 words |
| 7 | 18 | **First soft pitch** | the offer mentioned for the first time — context, not pressure | 350-500 words |
| 8 | 22 | **Objection handling** | addresses the most common reason people hesitate | 350-500 words |
| 9 | 26 | **Stronger pitch** | full offer breakdown, why now, what's included | 400-600 words |
| 10 | 30 | **Decision frame** | "here's where this goes from here" — not closed, but framed | 250-400 words |

## Psychology of the arc

The evergreen is a slow launch. The reader doesn't know it's a sales sequence until email 7. By then they've gotten 6 emails of genuine value and they've bonded with [VOICE OWNER]. The pitch lands because it's earned.

Critical sequencing:
- **Emails 1-3 = identity and ideas.** Reader is deciding whether [VOICE OWNER] is worth reading.
- **Emails 4-6 = stories and proof.** Reader is building belief that the work delivers.
- **Emails 7-10 = ask, but on slow burn.** Each email reduces a different reason to not buy.

## Rules

- **Don't pitch before email 7.** Pitching too early signals "this was a sales sequence all along" and the trust frame collapses.
- **Pacing matters more than length.** 30 days is the floor for trust to build. Compressing to 14 days hurts conversion.
- **Stop on purchase.** Buyer immediately exits the sequence and enters the post-purchase sequence. Connector must enforce this.
- **Stop on enrollment in any other sequence.** Don't run evergreen and launch in parallel.
- **One offer per evergreen sequence.** If the business has 3 offers, build 3 evergreen sequences and segment by interest signal. Don't try to pitch all 3 in one sequence.
- **Test the "first pitch" email obsessively.** Email 7 is the most important conversion email — small changes to it move the whole sequence's revenue.

## Variants

**Short evergreen (5 emails / 14 days):** for low-ticket products ($7-$97) where the lifetime value doesn't justify a 30-day arc. Compress to: identity → idea → proof → pitch → close.

**Long evergreen (15-20 emails / 60-90 days):** for high-ticket ($1k+) or considered purchases (B2B SaaS, coaching). More teaching, more proof, more time for the offer to feel inevitable.

**Indoctrination-only (5 emails / 7 days):** if the business doesn't have a direct-response offer and lives on long-term LTV (newsletter, community, consulting), the evergreen can stop at email 5 and dump the reader into a permanent broadcast list.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Why it kills conversion |
|---------|------------------------|
| Pitching in email 2 or 3 | Trust hasn't built; reader assumes the whole sequence is a sales funnel |
| All 10 emails feel like content marketing | No clear ask = no clear conversion |
| Email 7 first-pitch is meek | Mentioning the offer in one sentence ≠ pitching — the pitch needs full air |
| Email 9 stronger-pitch is identical to email 7 | Reader thinks "you already showed me this" and ignores |
| No exit on purchase | Customer who buys keeps getting pitched the thing they just bought |
| Running evergreen in parallel with a launch | Email cadence collisions destroy both |

## Connector requirements

- Trigger on opt-in or low-ticket purchase
- Halt on core-offer purchase
- Halt if subscriber enters launch sequence
- Personalize on which lead magnet / tripwire brought them in (changes the email-1 framing)
- Track revenue per email so future iterations can rebalance the arc
