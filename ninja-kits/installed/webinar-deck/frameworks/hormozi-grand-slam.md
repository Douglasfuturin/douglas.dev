# Alex Hormozi, Grand Slam Offer Framework

> The dominant offer construction framework for B2B services, fitness, agencies, and premium info-products since ~2022. First published in $100M Offers (2021), expanded in $100M Leads (2023). Built on direct response classics (Eugene Schwartz, Dan Kennedy) and refined for the era of paid social.

## Core insight

Most offer creators focus on **what to sell**. The Grand Slam Offer focuses on making the prospect feel that the perceived value is so much higher than the price that NOT buying feels stupid.

Hormozi's framework is offer-construction-first, story-second. Where Brunson moves you with narrative, Hormozi moves you with math and proof. They're not opposites, the best modern webinars use both, but the center of gravity is different.

## The Value Equation

The foundation of everything Hormozi teaches.

```
              Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
   VALUE  =  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                       Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

Four variables:

| Variable | Definition | How to improve |
|----------|------------|----------------|
| **Dream Outcome** | What they want at the end | Make it more specific, more vivid, more emotionally resonant |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | Their belief it will work | Stack proof: case studies, screenshots, specific numbers |
| **Time Delay** | How long until they see results | Shorten it, OR show early wins inside the long journey |
| **Effort & Sacrifice** | What they have to do / give up | Reduce it: done-for-you, templates, scripts, support |

**The leverage is in the denominator.** Most offer-builders only work on the numerator (bigger dream, more proof). The 10x offers reduce Time Delay and Effort & Sacrifice.

**Weaker:** "Get rich in 5 years if you work 3 hours a day."
**Stronger:** "Get your first $10k client in 6 weeks without writing a single line of code."

The frame is: make the math obvious. Don't make the prospect calculate value, show them.

## The 5 pieces of a Grand Slam Offer

1. **Problems → Solutions.** List every problem the avatar faces in achieving the outcome. Each problem becomes a solution. Each solution becomes a deliverable inside the offer.

2. **Delivery Vehicles.** For each solution, decide how it gets delivered (video module, 1-on-1, group call, software, done-for-you template, community access). Stacking delivery vehicles increases perceived value without proportional cost.

3. **Bundle.** Combine into ONE offer with ONE price.

4. **Enhance.** Add bonuses, guarantee, scarcity, urgency, naming. Each adds value perception without adding cost.

5. **Name.** Magnetic, specific, aspirational.

The output of this exercise is a list of 10-30 line items that go into the Stack slide.

## Bonuses solve specific objections

This is the single most replicated Hormozi rule. Bonuses are NOT "extra stuff." Each bonus solves ONE specific objection the prospect has.

**The exercise:**
1. List the top 10 reasons someone would NOT buy
2. For each one, design a bonus that eliminates it
3. Each bonus gets its own line in the Stack

Examples:
- Objection: "I don't know how to start" → Bonus: "First 7 Days" quick-start guide
- Objection: "What if I get stuck?" → Bonus: 12 months of Slack support
- Objection: "I don't have time to figure out X" → Bonus: Done-for-you X template
- Objection: "Will this work for my niche?" → Bonus: Niche-specific case study library

When a bonus has no objection it's solving, cut it. Adding bonuses to "build value" doesn't work, adding bonuses to "kill objections" does.

## The MAGIC offer naming formula

- **M, Magic outcome** (specific, vivid)
- **A, Avatar** (who specifically)
- **G, Goal** (what specifically)
- **I, Interval** (timeframe)
- **C, Container** (how it's delivered)

**Strong:** "The 5-Day Email Lead Machine: How Local Dentists Generate 40 New Patients/Month Without Ads"
**Weak:** "Marketing for Dentists", generic, no avatar specificity, no goal, no interval.

Magnetic naming is the difference between an offer that gets ignored and one that gets opted-in instantly. The avatar should be able to read the name and feel "that's me." If the name applies to 100 different niches, it's too generic.

## Risk reversal, Hormozi's signature weapon

Hormozi pushes guarantees harder than anyone in modern direct response. The structure:

**Conditional guarantee:**
> "If you do X by Y date and don't get Z result, I'll {refund / double your money / work with you for free until you do}."

The condition matters because:
1. It self-selects buyers who actually believe they'll do the work
2. It transfers all risk to the seller, making the buyer's decision rational
3. The few buyers who claim are usually the ones who couldn't have succeeded anyway

**Better than money-back: results-based.**
> "If you don't make 10x your investment in 12 months, I'll refund your money AND give you another $1,000."

Sounds insane. Works because:
- Self-selects believers
- Eliminates risk completely from buyer's side
- Few people actually claim if the program delivers

**The math:** if your program closes 30% more sales because of an aggressive guarantee, and 5% of those new sales claim the guarantee, you net 24% more revenue with happier customers. The conventional "30-day money-back" leaves money on the table.

**Caveat:** the guarantee must be sustainable. Promising things you can't deliver = legal liability, refund processing nightmares, reputation damage. Run the numbers before designing it.

## Hormozi VSL / Webinar structure

Hormozi-style webinars run 60-75 min. Less story-heavy than Brunson, more proof-heavy. Common section structure:

| # | Section | Time | Purpose |
|---|---------|------|---------|
| 1 | Hook | 1-3 min | Specific promise + who it's for |
| 2 | Promise | 3-5 min | What you'll teach today + why it matters now |
| 3 | Plan | 5-10 min | Overview of the framework (often named acronym) |
| 4 | Proof | 10-15 min | Case studies, screenshots, specific numbers |
| 5 | Pain | 5-10 min | Cost of not solving (financial, emotional, time) |
| 6 | Process | 15-25 min | Actual teaching of the framework |
| 7 | Pitch | 10-15 min | Grand Slam Offer + stack |
| 8 | Push | 5-10 min | Urgency, scarcity, close |

Key differences from Brunson:
- Less story → more proof / specificity
- Framework named with an acronym (MAGIC, CLOSER, GSO, etc.)
- Value equation often shown explicitly on slides
- Risk reversal is the closing weapon (vs. Brunson's stack drama)
- Tone is transactional and clinical, less seductive, more confident

## The CLOSER framework (for booked-call follow-ups)

From $100M Leads. Useful when the webinar funnel includes a 1-on-1 sales call after:

- **C**, Clarify why they're here
- **L**, Label the problem they have
- **O**, Overview their past pain
- **S**, Sell them the vacation (the vision of the solved future)
- **E**, Explain away concerns
- **R**, Reinforce the decision

Not a webinar structure on its own, but if your webinar drives applications to a call, the closer team should run CLOSER on every call.

## Specificity over claims

Hormozi's #1 copy principle: replace claims with specifics.

| Don't say | Say |
|-----------|-----|
| Lots of money | $2.4M in 9 months |
| Many clients | 143 clients across 11 industries |
| Quickly | In 17 days |
| Most people | 89% of our students |
| Some results | 47x ROI on ad spend |

This requires real data. If you don't have it, your webinar will feel like every other one. The brands winning with Hormozi-style webinars in 2026 are the ones that tracked metrics from day one and can put real screenshots on slides.

**On the deck:** every claim slide should have a specific number. Every case study slide should have a date, a name, a number, and ideally a screenshot. Vague proof slides are worse than no proof slides.

## Common Hormozi-style mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails |
|---------|--------------|
| Treating the value equation as a literal math equation prospects calculate | They don't calculate it, it's a frame for you, not them. Don't ask them to do the math. |
| Building Grand Slam Offers from scratch with no buyer feedback | Offers improve via iteration, not theory. Test on real buyers first. |
| Risk reversals so aggressive they feel desperate | "100x guarantee!", buyers smell the desperation |
| Acronym overuse | Every framework named MAGIC / CLOSER / GSO eventually feels gimmicky |
| Stripping all story out | Hormozi's own webinars have stories, he just doesn't lead with them. Pure-proof webinars feel cold. |
| Specificity without proof | Claiming "$2.4M in 9 months" with no screenshot = legal liability + trust collapse |
| Inflated values on stack lines | A "$50,000 value!" line item nobody believes destroys the whole stack |

## Where Hormozi works better than Brunson

- Premium / B2B audiences who hate the Brunson "stack slide" aesthetic
- Service businesses where proof is verifiable (agencies, fitness, finance)
- Audiences over 35 who've seen the Brunson pattern too many times
- High-ticket ($5k+) where the buyer wants math, not story
- Audiences that pride themselves on being "rational" (engineers, finance, ops)

## Where Brunson still wins

- Lifestyle, personal-development, online business, fitness verticals
- $497-$1,997 price points where the stack drama lands
- Audiences under 30 who don't have the pattern history
- Female-skewing audiences (story still moves)
- Live (not evergreen) webinars where chat energy matters

## The composite move

The best modern webinars (2024-2026) blend both:

- **Narrative spine** from Brunson (Hook, Story, 3 Secrets, Stack, 3 Closes)
- **Proof density** from Hormozi (specific numbers, screenshots, named frameworks)
- **Stack slide** reframed as a value-equation visualization (denominator shrinks, numerator grows)
- **Risk reversal** from Hormozi folded into Brunson's "Close 2: Risk"
- **Modern minimalist visual language**, black backgrounds, big type, one accent color, no bullet points

This is what `composite-150-slide.md` builds.
