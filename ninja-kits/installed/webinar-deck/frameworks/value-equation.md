# Hormozi's Value Equation, Applied to Webinar Decks

> The math behind why some offers feel like obvious deals and others feel like a stretch. From $100M Offers (2021). Used as the analytical scaffolding behind the composite framework's offer construction.

## The equation

```
              Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
   VALUE  =  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                       Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

Four variables. Three are intuitive (dream outcome, time, effort). One is the lever most people miss (perceived likelihood).

## The four variables, applied to your offer

### Dream Outcome (numerator)

What does the prospect want at the end?

**Strong dream outcomes are:**
- Specific (an amount, a metric, a named state)
- Vivid (the prospect can picture it)
- Identity-aligned (matches who they want to become)

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| "Grow your business" | "Hit $50k/month in recurring revenue" |
| "Lose weight" | "Drop 20 pounds and fit into your old jeans" |
| "Make more money" | "Take home an extra $10k/month from the side project you've been sitting on" |

The webinar's slide 2 (the one-line promise) should be the Dream Outcome stated cleanly.

### Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (numerator)

Their belief that it will work, for them, specifically.

This is the leverage variable most offer-builders miss. You can have a huge dream outcome, but if the prospect doesn't believe they'll achieve it, the value collapses.

**Levers to increase perceived likelihood:**
- Case studies with people who look like the prospect (same age, niche, starting point)
- Specific numbers in proof ("$2.4M in 9 months" not "lots of money")
- Screenshots of real results (Stripe dashboards, before/after, analytics)
- Aggregate stats ("89% of students who completed module 3 hit X")
- Risk reversal (if you don't get there, money back)
- Step-by-step process visualization (it looks doable)
- Anti-fluff positioning ("this isn't about working harder, here's exactly what changed")

The 3 Secrets section + Proof slides exist almost entirely to increase Perceived Likelihood.

### Time Delay (denominator)

How long until the prospect sees results?

Decreasing Time Delay = increasing value, even if Dream Outcome stays constant.

**Levers to decrease perceived Time Delay:**
- Quick wins inside the longer journey ("first result in week 1")
- Specific timeline commitments ("First client in 30 days")
- Pre-built templates that compress execution time
- Pre-recorded content + live calls (no waiting for next cohort)
- Office hours / fast support to unblock quickly

**Critical move:** even if the full Dream Outcome takes 12 months, name the EARLY win and put it in week 1 or 2. The prospect's brain pattern-matches "I'll see results soon" instead of "I'll see results eventually."

### Effort & Sacrifice (denominator)

What do they have to do, give up, learn, change?

Decreasing Effort = increasing value.

**Levers to decrease perceived Effort & Sacrifice:**
- Done-for-you templates (they don't have to create from scratch)
- Scripts they can copy-paste
- Software / automation that removes manual work
- Group format instead of solo (they're not figuring it out alone)
- Accountability built in (they don't have to manufacture discipline)
- Pre-built workflows / SOPs

**Critical move:** describe the effort in honest, low-friction language. "20 minutes a day" beats "as much as it takes." Specific commitments feel small; vague commitments feel large.

## Using the equation to design the offer

**Step 1.** Write down the prospect's Dream Outcome in one specific sentence.

**Step 2.** Brainstorm 20+ levers across all four variables. Force quantity.

**Step 3.** Cluster levers into:
- Core thing (the main vehicle)
- Bonuses that increase Perceived Likelihood
- Bonuses that decrease Time Delay
- Bonuses that decrease Effort & Sacrifice

**Step 4.** Each bonus must also solve a specific objection (see `stack-slide-anatomy.md`). The intersection of "increases value equation" + "kills a specific objection" is the strongest bonus design.

**Step 5.** Compute the qualitative ratio. Does the resulting value feel 10x the price? If not, add more denominator-reducing bonuses.

## Using the equation to design the deck

The 150-slide deck increases each variable at specific moments:

| Section | Equation variable it improves |
|---------|------------------------------|
| Section 1 (Cold open promise) | Dream Outcome named clearly |
| Section 2 (Who Am I, proof) | Perceived Likelihood |
| Section 3 (Big Domino) | Perceived Likelihood (reframes what's possible) |
| Section 4 (Origin Story) | Perceived Likelihood (if it happened to them, it can happen to me) |
| Section 5-7 (3 Secrets) | Perceived Likelihood (each secret = belief installation) |
| Section 9 (Offer Reveal) | Dream Outcome + Container clarity |
| Section 10 (Stack) | Time + Effort (each bonus shows decreased time / effort) |
| Section 11 Close 1 (Money/ROI) | Math makes Dream Outcome concrete |
| Section 11 Close 2 (Risk) | Perceived Likelihood (if it doesn't work, no loss) |
| Section 11 Close 3 (Time) | Decision velocity (urgency reduces time-to-decide) |

Every slide should be auditable against: "which equation variable does this improve?" If a slide doesn't improve any variable, cut it.

## Naming the offer using MAGIC

The offer name is a compressed expression of the value equation.

- **M**, Magic outcome (Dream Outcome stated vividly)
- **A**, Avatar (who specifically, drives Perceived Likelihood)
- **G**, Goal (what specifically)
- **I**, Interval (Time Delay, shorter = stronger)
- **C**, Container (delivery vehicle, implies Effort level)

Pass an offer name through MAGIC:
- "Marketing for Dentists", fails (no specific outcome, no interval, no container)
- "The 5-Day Email Lead Machine: How Local Dentists Generate 40 New Patients/Month Without Ads", passes all 5

Slide 116 (offer name) should pass MAGIC. If it doesn't, the deck's center of gravity is missing.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Effect on value equation |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Focusing only on Dream Outcome | Ignores Perceived Likelihood, Time, and Effort, the levers with more headroom |
| Vague language ("transform your life", "elevate your business") | Doesn't move any variable, could mean anything |
| Pile of bonuses with no objection / variable mapping | Each bonus is supposed to improve the equation, pile-on dilutes |
| Claiming massive Dream Outcome with no Perceived Likelihood support | Equation collapses; reads as hype |
| Time Delay = "as long as it takes" | Implicit infinity in the denominator → value approaches zero |
| Effort = "you'll work hard" | Most prospects discount this aggressively, "I won't" |

## When the equation is the wrong tool

The value equation is a framework for analytical buyers. Some audiences buy on feel and pattern, not math:

- Lifestyle / personal-development audiences often buy from identity match, not ROI math
- Female-skewing audiences in coaching / wellness often buy from story bond, not value calculation
- Sub-25 audiences haven't been pitched enough to be cynical, story works, math is unnecessary

For these audiences, use the equation as YOUR analytical tool during offer design, but don't put the equation on slides. Show the math through the stack and the 3 closes instead.

Hormozi's own webinars do this, the equation shows up in writing (books, training) but rarely on his actual sales decks. The math designed the offer. The deck delivers the math through stories and stacks.
