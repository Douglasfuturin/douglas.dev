# The 3 Closes, Money, Risk, Time

> Slides 141-148 in the composite framework. The three sequential objection-killers that close the gap between "this looks good" and "I'm buying right now."

## Why three closes (not one)

Every prospect has at least three reasons not to buy at the moment of decision:

1. **Money**, "Can I afford this? Is it worth it?"
2. **Risk**, "What if it doesn't work?"
3. **Time**, "Why now instead of later?"

A single close that tries to address all three feels desperate and incoherent. Three sequential closes, each handling one objection cleanly, feel professional and complete.

The order matters: Money → Risk → Time. Money first because it's the most rational objection. Risk second because once price is justified, risk becomes the next gate. Time last because urgency only works after the buyer has decided the offer is worth it and safe to take.

---

## Close 1, Money (slides 141-142)

### Goal
Make the math obvious. Move them from "this costs $X" to "this is a no-brainer ROI."

### Slide 141: ROI calculation

Show the math explicitly:

```
Investment: $2,997
First client at $2k/mo = $24,000/yr
That's a 700% return, in year 1 alone.
```

Or for a non-direct-revenue offer:

```
Investment: $1,497
Time saved: 5 hrs/week × 50 weeks = 250 hrs
At your hourly value of $100, that's $25,000.
Pays for itself in week 3.
```

The math must be defensible. Don't invent the "your hourly value", ask the audience to fill it in, or pick a conservative number their avatar would agree to.

### Slide 142: Cost of NOT solving

Annualize the pain. Compound the loss.

```
Don't do it →
Another 12 months of $0 in new clients.
Another 12 months of the same problem.
Another year you can't get back.
```

Or for B2B:

```
Don't do it →
$X/year still leaking from the same hole.
The team still working around it.
Compounding into year 2, year 3, year 4.
```

The "cost of inaction" frame works because most buyers compute the cost of buying but not the cost of NOT buying. You make it visible.

## Close 2, Risk (slides 143-144)

### Goal
Eliminate the buyer's perceived risk. Transfer it to you.

### Slide 143: The guarantee headline

The guarantee belongs on its own slide. Big text. Specific.

```
The guarantee:
If you don't [specific result] in [interval],
I'll [specific remedy].
```

Examples:
- "If you don't book 3 sales calls in 30 days, I'll refund every dollar."
- "If you don't see measurable progress in 60 days, I'll work with you 1-on-1 until you do, at no extra charge."
- "If you don't make 10x your investment in 12 months, I'll refund AND give you $1,000."

The conditional ("if you do X by Y date") matters. It self-selects buyers who plan to do the work, and signals that the seller stands behind the offer with skin in the game.

### Slide 144: Guarantee details

Spell out the terms, but on ONE slide, in plain language. Long terms in fine print look hedged; short terms in big text look confident.

```
Here's how it works:
1. Show up to every live call for the first 30 days.
2. Submit your homework on time.
3. If you don't see [result], email support@company and we refund 100%.
```

If the conditions are reasonable and the buyer knows they'll meet them, the guarantee is effectively "no risk." That's the conversion lever.

### Stronger variants

| Variant | Mechanism |
|---------|-----------|
| **Results-based** | "If you don't get [specific result], full refund" |
| **Double money-back** | "If you don't get X, I'll refund AND give you another $Y" |
| **Time-extended** | "If after 90 days you're not where you want to be, I'll work with you for another 90 at no charge" |
| **Performance-based** | "Pay 50% now, 50% only when you hit [result]" |
| **Conditional double** | "If you do X and don't see Y, I refund and pay you Z" |

The stronger the guarantee, the higher the conversion, AND the more it self-selects committed buyers. Few will actually claim if the offer delivers.

**Caveat:** the guarantee must be operationally sustainable. Don't promise things you can't process. Refund logistics, legal compliance, and reputation damage from broken guarantees are all worse than not offering one.

## Close 3, Time (slides 145-146)

### Goal
Move them from "I should do this someday" to "I should do this RIGHT NOW."

### Slide 145: Urgency reason

The urgency must be REAL. Manufactured scarcity ("only 5 spots left!" when there are 500) is the fastest way to destroy a brand's long-term sales engine.

**The primary urgency source is the webinar-only promotional price** (see the dedicated section below). It is always present, always legitimate, and should anchor Close 3. The sources below stack on top of it when they're genuinely true:
- **Cohort capacity:** "We only take 30 students per cohort and the next one closes Thursday"
- **Bonus expiry:** "The Done-For-You templates ($1,497 value) are only included if you join in the next 48 hours"
- **Price increase:** "The price goes from $1,997 to $2,997 on Friday at midnight, this is the last call at the lower price"
- **Live access window:** "I'm running this live for the next 90 days, then it goes evergreen with no Q&A"
- **External deadline:** "Q1 starts in 3 weeks, if you want this in place before then, today's the day"

```
Why now:
The next cohort closes Thursday at midnight.
After that, we don't open again until [date].
```

### Slide 146: Specific deadline

```
Closes Thursday 11:59pm PT.
After that the bonuses disappear.
After that the price goes up.
```

Specific time + specific consequence. Both must be true and enforced.

**Enforcement matters.** If the deadline passes and the bonuses still show up "as a special exception," your future urgency is dead. The audience now knows the deadlines are flexible. This effect compounds across launches.

## Webinar-only promotional pricing

Every webinar carries a special promotional price, distinct from the standard price, available only to people who attended (or who act within a set window after). This is the single cleanest source of *legitimate* urgency, and it should be present in every webinar by default.

**Why it works:**
- The urgency is real. The promo genuinely expires, you're not inventing scarcity, you're offering a real, time-bound deal.
- It rewards the action you want: showing up, and deciding now.
- It gives the close a concrete reason to act today instead of "someday."
- It protects the standard price. People who didn't attend still pay full freight, so the offer's everyday value isn't eroded.

**How to structure it:**
- **Standard price**, what the offer normally costs. State it FIRST, as the anchor.
- **Webinar price**, the promotional price, clearly framed as "because you're here, today."
- **The window**, a real deadline: end of the webinar, 24 hours, 72 hours, or until the next cohort fills. Pick one and enforce it.
- **Enforce it.** If the deadline passes and the webinar price still works, every future webinar's urgency is dead. The discipline compounds.

On the deck this maps to slides 137-138 (reveal) and 145-146 (the deadline close):

```
The standard price: $X,XXX
                         ↓
Because you're here today: $X
This price holds until [real deadline].
```

**For application-funnel webinars** (where the CTA is "book a call," not "buy now"): the promo still applies. Frame it as "attendees who book a strategy call before [deadline] lock in the webinar pricing." The exact number gets confirmed on the call, but the urgency, and the reason to book NOW instead of later, is established here on the webinar.

## Action plan + button (slides 147-148)

### Slide 147: 3-step action plan

Reduce decision friction by spelling out the next 3 steps:

```
Right now:
1. Click the button below
2. Fill out the application (60 seconds)
3. Get instant access, start tonight
```

### Slide 148: Button slide

URL, big. Hero typography.

```
[CompanyName].com/joinnow
```

Hold this slide for the entire Q&A / final block. It's the call-to-action that stays on screen while you handle live objections.

## Pacing across the 3 closes

| Slide | Hold time |
|-------|-----------|
| 141, Money ROI | 20-30 sec, walk the math aloud |
| 142, Cost of NOT | 15-20 sec, punch the loss |
| 143, Guarantee headline | 15-20 sec, let it land |
| 144, Guarantee details | 30-45 sec, walk the conditions |
| 145, Urgency reason | 15-20 sec |
| 146, Deadline | 15-20 sec |
| 147, Action plan | 20-30 sec, read all 3 steps |
| 148, Button | 5-10 min (held during FAQ/close) |

Total: ~6 minutes of presented content, then 5-10 minutes of live Q&A with the button slide held.

## Common 3-close mistakes

| Mistake | Why it kills conversion |
|---------|------------------------|
| Combining money + risk + time onto one slide | Cognitive overload; each objection deserves its own moment |
| Money close before the offer is fully named | They don't know what they're calculating ROI for |
| Guarantee that's pity-based instead of confidence-based | "Money-back if you're not happy", too soft, signals seller's doubt |
| Manufactured urgency | Reputation damage compounds across all future launches |
| Action plan with more than 3 steps | Decision friction defeats the close |
| Button slide that's small / buried | The CTA must be the largest visual element on the screen |
| Closing immediately after stack, no breathing room | Stack is overwhelming; needs 1 transition slide before closes |
| No FAQ slide after the closes | Last-mile objection-killer left on the table |

## When to fold in Hormozi's value equation

If your audience leans rational / B2B / over-35, replace Close 1 (Money) with a value-equation slide:

```
Dream outcome:       Get 10 clients/month
Likelihood:          47x ROAS proven across 143 students
Time:                90 days, not 18 months
Effort:              Done-for-you templates, no writing required
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
That's a 50x-200x value-to-price ratio.
```

This replaces the more emotional "cost of inaction" framing with explicit math. Works better for rational audiences; weaker for lifestyle/personal-development audiences who buy on feel.

The composite framework allows either, pick based on the avatar.
