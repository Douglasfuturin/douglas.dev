# Marketing Psychology Cheat Sheet

Every section of a landing page should activate at least one of these principles. Most great direct-response pages stack 3-5 simultaneously.

## The seven engines

### 1. Social proof
"If smart people like me are doing this, it must be the right move."

Activators:
- Named testimonials with photo + specific result ("Sarah, real estate coach in Austin — went from $0 to $34K MRR in 90 days")
- Logo wall of recognizable client brands
- Screenshot proofs (Stripe earnings, calendar bookings, DM screenshots)
- "Join 4,217 founders" counter
- Trustpilot/G2/Capterra badges with real rating + review count
- Recent activity ("Tom from Denver just joined")

**Pitfalls**: Anonymous testimonials, round-number social proof ("Join 1,000+"), stock photos.

### 2. Authority
"This person has the right to teach me this."

Activators:
- Named credentials ("Former Head of Growth at Stripe")
- Press logos ("As seen in Forbes, Inc, Entrepreneur")
- Founder portrait next to bio with specific achievements
- Quantified track record ("$47M in client revenue generated")
- Affiliations ("Trusted by teams at Y Combinator, Andreessen Horowitz")
- Books written, talks given, podcast guest appearances

**Pitfalls**: Vague authority ("industry leader"), unverifiable claims.

### 3. Scarcity
"If I don't move now, I'll miss it."

Activators:
- Cohort start date + countdown
- Hard seat cap ("Only 200 spots — 47 remaining")
- Deadline ("Pricing increases in 24 hours")
- Time-limited bonus ("Today only")
- Closing waitlist
- Founding-member pricing window

**Pitfalls**: Fake scarcity (countdown that resets), permanent "limited time" offers — destroys trust if visitor returns.

### 4. Reciprocity
"They gave me something valuable; I owe them attention."

Activators:
- Free training, PDF, audit, mini-course as lead magnet
- A free chapter or first module unlocked
- Hyper-specific giveaway (template, swipe file, calculator)
- Long-form value in the VSL itself (teach something useful even if they don't buy)

**Pitfalls**: Generic "free guide" with no specificity. The gift must feel valuable.

### 5. Commitment / consistency
"I already started — I should finish."

Activators:
- Multi-step form (email first → small question → bigger question → call booking)
- "Yes/No" survey questions that get the visitor saying yes 3+ times before the offer
- Quiz funnels that personalize the offer based on answers
- Micro-commitments before the big ask ("Take the 60-second assessment")
- Public commitment ("I'm joining the 5-day challenge")

**Pitfalls**: Too many steps before any value delivered. Cap at 3-5 micro-commitments.

### 6. Liking
"I want to work with this person."

Activators:
- Founder video that's casual, well-lit, authentic (not over-produced)
- Personal backstory ("Three years ago I was broke too…")
- Shared identity ("This is for fellow {tribe}")
- Humor / personality in copy (most LPs are sterile — break the pattern)
- Pet/family photo somewhere subtle (humanizes the founder)

**Pitfalls**: Trying too hard to be relatable. Authenticity beats polish.

### 7. Unity (Cialdini's 7th — shared identity)
"They're one of us."

Activators:
- "For agency owners who hate ad-spend roulette"
- "Built by founders, for founders"
- Insider language only the target audience uses
- Calling out the audience's specific pain in their own words
- Anti-positioning against the out-group ("Stop being a $97 course hopper")

**Pitfalls**: Over-narrowing — the audience must still feel large enough to be a real opportunity.

## Pattern stacks (used together)

**The "VSL stack"**: Authority (founder cred) → Reciprocity (value in the video itself) → Social proof (testimonials below video) → Scarcity (cohort deadline) → Risk reversal (guarantee) → Commitment (book a call as micro-commitment).

**The "challenge funnel stack"**: Unity (calling out the tribe) → Scarcity (cohort start date + seat cap) → Social proof (testimonials from past cohorts with named results) → Authority (instructor cred) → Reciprocity (the 5-day challenge IS valuable on its own).

**The "application page stack"**: Authority (founder cred up top) → Social proof (logo wall + testimonial right under hero) → Unity ("if you're a serious {tribe} member, apply") → Commitment (multi-step application form — qualifying is itself a commitment).

## The skim test

For each section, ask: **"If a visitor only read the headlines + looked at the images, would they still be moved toward the CTA?"** If no, the section is dead weight.

## The friction test

For each section, ask: **"Does this raise or lower the visitor's cortisol?"** Lower it with: trust, specificity, social proof, photos of real humans, named results. Raise it (productively, to create motion) with: scarcity, "you might lose this", problem agitation.

## Loss aversion is more powerful than gain framing

People will work 2x as hard to avoid losing $100 as they will to gain $100. Reframe gain language into loss language wherever possible.

- "Get more leads" → "Stop watching leads slip through your fingers"
- "Build a recurring offer" → "Stop chasing one-off projects"
- "Make $10K/month" → "Stop trading hours for dollars while your competitors automate"

## Specificity beats hype

| Hype | Specificity |
|------|-------------|
| Massive results | $47K MRR in 90 days |
| Tons of clients | 217 paying clients |
| Industry-leading | 4.9/5 across 312 Trustpilot reviews |
| Best-in-class | Higher LTV than the Salesforce CRM benchmark |

Always write the specific version. Use odd, un-round numbers. They feel real because they ARE real.
