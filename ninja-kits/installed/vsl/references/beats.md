# Beats In Depth

Each of the 11 beats with its job, the formulas that work, the transitions in and out, and before/after rewrites drawn from real VSLs in the agency / coaching / info-product space.

---

## Beat 1 — The Hook (0:00-0:30)

### Job
Stop the scroll, name the audience, make a specific promise that the rest of the video pays off.

### Formulas

**1.1 The "what if I told you" hook**
> "What if I told you that you could [outcome] in [timeframe], without [obvious downside 1], without [obvious downside 2], and without [obvious downside 3]?"

Loads up the disqualifier ("without") clauses to short-circuit the visitor's initial skepticism. Three "withouts" is the sweet spot — two feels under-built, four feels desperate.

**1.2 The direct outcome + guarantee hook**
> "We'll [specific outcome] for you, or you don't pay."

Used by views-to-clients literally in the first sentence: "We'll grow your business' YouTube channel completely done for you to generate leads and sales, or you don't pay." Names the deliverable, names the guarantee, sets the entire frame in 14 words.

**1.3 The "if you" tribe-naming hook**
> "If you [specific situation only the tribe is in], I want to talk to you about something that is probably on your mind right now."

Used by studio-flow: "If you run a marketing or advertising agency, I want to talk to you about something that is probably on your mind right now. AI creative production." Names the tribe, names the topic, no preamble.

**1.4 The pain-aware opener**
> "If you're done [common painful behavior], fighting [external force pushing back], and struggling to [aspirational outcome they can't reach], this video will explain to you exactly why."

Used by passionate-few: "If you're done spending money on ads that don't convert, fighting rising CPMs, and struggling to earn real trust with your audience, this video will explain to you exactly why." Acknowledges their fatigue and promises the answer.

**1.5 The category-creation hook**
> "[Big claim about a new category that doesn't yet exist]. Here's what it is and why it matters."

Higher risk, higher reward. Only works if the category claim is genuinely contrarian.

### Anti-patterns

- "Hi everyone, my name is..." → throat-clearing
- "In this video, I'm going to..." → meta-commentary instead of the thing itself
- "Today I want to talk about..." → no urgency, no specific tribe
- "Are you struggling with X?" → rhetorical question hooks are weak; just NAME the struggle

### Transition out
End the hook with one of:
- "In the next [N] minutes, I'm going to show you exactly how this works."
- "Stick with me, and I'll show you how this works, the results you can expect, and what's required from you."
- A hard cut to beat 2 with no transition (sometimes the cleanest move).

## Beat 2 — Promise + Proof Up Front (0:30-1:30)

### Job
Make the visitor believe you have the right to keep talking. State the specific outcome you produce, and immediately attach a name + number to it.

### Formulas

**2.1 The triple-stack promise**
> "I'm going to show you three things: [outcome 1], [outcome 2], and [outcome 3]."

Three is the magic number. Two feels light, four feels like overpromising.

**2.2 The "who I am, here's why I have the right" mini-credentials drop**
> "My name is [name]. I've [specific track record], and what I'm about to show you is the biggest opportunity I've seen in my entire career."

Frontloaded credentials with a strong claim. Save the deeper authority bar for later (around beat 8 or wherever the visitor needs to be re-convinced).

**2.3 The named-client salvo (skip ahead to proof immediately)**
> "Sam went from 0 to 40,000 subscribers in 10 months. Brian went from 0 to 1,000 subscribers and added $20,000 in MRR in 25 days. Glen Coco went from 0 to 10,000 subscribers and closed a $45,000 deal in his first 60 days."

Used by views-to-clients. Pure proof, no preamble. Names + specific numbers + specific timeframes. Lands hard for cold traffic that doesn't yet trust the speaker.

### Anti-patterns
- "I've helped lots of people get great results" → zero specificity
- "I've been doing this for years" → "years" is not a number
- Vague brag-stack with no client names → "we've worked with thousands" is weaker than "Luke went from $30k loss to $201k revenue in 30 days"

## Beat 3 — Problem Agitation (1:30-2:30)

### Job
Name the visitor's current state in specific, visceral terms. Make them feel seen. Validate that they've tried things and those things didn't work.

### Formulas

**3.1 The "let me guess where you are right now" opener**
> "Let me guess where you are right now. You're [specific situation 1]. You're [specific situation 2]. And the worst part is [hidden cost they may not have named]."

Used by Client Ascension verbatim. Each clause MUST be specific — "working too much" is dead, "checking Stripe at 11pm hoping a charge came in" is alive.

**3.2 The three-pillar problem stack**
Cover three flavors of the same underlying problem:
- Tools/systems don't work together
- Team is buried in recurring work
- AI/tools they've tried haven't compounded

This is the most universal pain pattern across B2B service offers. Adapt the three pillars to the specific niche, but keep the three-beat rhythm.

**3.3 The "and the math is brutal" beat**
After naming the pain, do the math out loud: "If you're making $75k a year and working 50 hours a week, that's $28 an hour — less than a lot of freelancers, but you can't quit because you need the money." Specific numbers make the pain undeniable.

**3.4 The "what you've tried" validator**
> "You've probably tried [X]. Either it was too complicated and you never started, or you started and realized it would take years, or you got stuck trading time for money again."

Pre-empts "I've tried things before". Names the three common failure modes and validates them as the visitor's reality, not their personal shortcoming.

### Transition out
> "And that's not what you want."
> "You don't want [current state]. You want [aspirational state]. That's what I'm going to show you how to do."

This swivels from agitation into solution-frame WITHOUT yet revealing the solution.

## Beat 4 — Identity-Crisis Reframe (2:30-3:00)

### Job
Reveal that the visitor's CURRENT GOAL is the trap. Pivot the entire frame from "I want more X" to "I've been chasing the wrong thing."

This is the most under-used beat in amateur VSLs. It's what moves the visitor from "shopping" to "I need to rethink this."

### Formula

> "Most [tribe] trade [thing they think they want] for [hidden cost]. More [X] = more [Y] = more [Z] = no [thing they actually want]. You're not building a [aspiration]. You're just [grim reframe of their current trajectory]."

### Real example (Client Ascension)
> "More clients just meant more work. You didn't escape. You just created another job for yourself, and that's not what you want."

In 22 words, the entire MMO industry's premise gets dismantled and the speaker becomes the only person with the real answer.

### When to use the reframe vs. skip it
- Skip on a tripwire / direct-purchase VSL — visitor is already 80% in
- Skip on warm-traffic application page VSL — visitor already self-identified
- Use heavily on cold-traffic challenge or high-ticket VSL — this is where you EARN the next 9 minutes

### Anti-patterns
- "You think you need X but actually you need Y" → too on-the-nose, sounds salesy
- Generic "you're chasing the wrong dream" → visitor doesn't believe you
- More than 30-45 seconds spent here → reframe is a pivot, not a destination

## Beat 5 — Mechanism Reveal (3:00-4:30)

### Job
Name the proprietary system or method. Position against alternatives. Give the visitor something to label, share, and remember.

### Formulas

**5.1 The named mechanism**
> "Here's what we built: [Memorable Named Method]. It's a [N-part / N-pillar] system that [does the thing in a specific way]."

Examples seen in the wild:
- "The KFC Method (Key, First click, Conversion)"
- "The AI Operating System"
- "The Overseer of YouTube" (an internal AI agent that becomes the named differentiator)
- "AI Creative Systems" (white-labeled and installed inside agencies)

The name should be:
- Easy to say in one breath
- Implies a specific function or asset
- NOT a generic adjective ("Premium System") or initials nobody can remember

**5.2 The "this is not" definition**
> "This is not [obvious alternative 1]. This is not [obvious alternative 2]. This is a [new category name]."

Used by studio-flow: "This is not just a course. This is not just a tool. This is a system that we install into your agency." Category-creation through negation.

**5.3 The "most people think X, but actually Y" reveal**
> "Most [tribe] think the problem is [common misdiagnosis]. The actual problem is [your diagnosis]. The actual solution is [your mechanism]."

Used by studio-flow: "It's not because you don't have the right tools or prompts. It's because you don't have a system." Reframes the visitor's diagnosis and slots your mechanism in as the answer.

### Anti-patterns
- "We use a proprietary framework" — undescribed proprietary frameworks signal nothing
- "Our process is special because we care more" — "we care more" is the worst possible differentiator
- Skipping the mechanism name entirely — without a name, your offer is a commodity

## Beat 6 — How It Works (4:30-6:00)

### Job
Show the visible path. What happens after they sign up, step by step, with low effort on their part emphasized.

### Formulas

**6.1 The phased install**
> "Phase 1: we install [thing]. Phase 2: we produce [proof]. Phase 3: we train your team."

Three phases, each with a concrete deliverable. Used by Studio Flow with the exact wording: "Phase 1 we install the system. Phase 2 we produce 100 custom ads done for you. Phase 3 we train your team."

**6.2 The numbered customer journey**
> "Step 1: you fill in a short onboarding doc. Step 2: we build [thing] inside [timeframe]. Step 3: you record [thing] using our guide. Step 4: it sells for you 24/7."

Used by Unorthodox Digital. Each step is short, concrete, and emphasizes that the heavy lifting is on their side, not the customer's.

**6.3 The "you only have to do X" simplifier**
> "Less than an hour a week. We handle everything: research, scripting, editing, thumbnails, optimization. The only part you do is [the irreducible piece]."

Used by views-to-clients to handle the "how much time will this take me" objection inside the process explanation.

### Time on this beat is risky
Most VSLs over-spend on mechanism + process and under-spend on proof and offer. If you're tight on time, this is the first beat to cut — the visitor needs to know it WORKS more than they need to know exactly HOW.

## Beat 7 — Results Stack (6:00-7:30)

### Job
Name 2-4 case studies with specific numbers and timeframes. Include at least one "typical" example and one "edge" example to pre-empt cherry-picking accusations.

### Formulas

**7.1 The triple-arc proof**
> "Here's what's possible. [Name] was a [background] who came to us after [bad state]. We [did the thing]. In the first [timeframe], they generated [specific outcome]. That's a [X-times] return."
>
> "Now I know what you're thinking — that's just one cherry-picked case. So let's look at typical. [Name 2] was a [background]. We built her [thing]. Inside 30 days she signed [specific number] clients at [specific price] from under [specific ad spend]."
>
> "And even our worst case is amazing. [Name 3] only ever lost money on paid ads before us. We built his [thing]. He signed a [specific number] contract from [tiny ad spend]."

This is the Unorthodox Digital pattern verbatim. Best-case, typical-case, worst-case — three named clients, each with a specific number, in under 90 seconds.

**7.2 The named-client roll-call** (front-loaded into beat 2, often)
> "Sam went from 0 to 40,000 in 10 months. Instantly added $1M ARR in 8 months. Levy crossed $1M/year in 8 months. Brook revived his dead channel to $45k/month in 4 months."

Pure list. No backstory. Each client gets one sentence. Lands when the audience trusts that you'd never make up named results.

### Anti-patterns
- Testimonials without names — "one of our clients..." is the weakest possible form
- Testimonials without numbers — "saw amazing results" is the second weakest
- Round numbers ($100k, 1000 customers) — feels invented; use $103,287 and 1,184
- Stacking 8+ case studies — past 4-5, the visitor stops absorbing; pick the strongest

## Beat 8 — Objection Handling (7:30-8:30)

### Job
Pre-empt the 2-4 objections that will kill the conversion. Name each objection in the visitor's words, then dismantle it.

### Formulas

**8.1 The "you might be thinking" opener**
> "Now you might be thinking — [objection in their words]. And I get it. [Validate the concern]. But here's the thing: [dismantle]."

Universal. Drop in anywhere mid-VSL.

**8.2 The pre-empted skepticism**
> "Before you ask if this is just AI — it isn't. Every script is built using a proven framework and worked on by me and my team."

Used by Unorthodox Digital. Names a specific 2026 objection (AI ghost-writing) and knocks it down with a real-people answer.

**8.3 The "I've been burned before" empathy beat**
> "Now you may also be thinking, I've been burned by agencies before. I've seen the ad, I've clicked, I didn't get the result. And look, I get it. So have we. But that's why [risk reversal you set up specifically to address this]."

Validates past pain + transitions directly into risk reversal.

### The four objections that show up in every B2B service VSL

1. **"This is just AI / a template / something I could find for free"** → answer with "we use [proprietary IP] + real humans + [specific quality control]"
2. **"How long will this take? I'm busy."** → answer with "[N hours/week] from you, we handle [extensive list of what we do]"
3. **"Will this work for my specific [niche / business model / situation]?"** → answer with named clients across multiple verticals
4. **"What if it doesn't work?"** → answer with risk reversal in the next beat

If your VSL doesn't address at least three of these four, it's leaking conversions.

## Beat 9 — Offer Reveal + Bonus Stack (8:30-9:15)

### Job
State exactly what they get, what it costs, and what the bonuses are worth. Make the price feel inevitable given the value just stacked.

### Formulas

**9.1 The "here's what you get" itemized list**
> "Here's what you get: [Deliverable 1, described as a problem solved]. [Deliverable 2, described as a problem solved]. [Deliverable 3, described as a problem solved]. [Bonus, with stated value]."

Frame each deliverable as a problem it solves, not a feature it has.

**9.2 The "the investment is" price reveal**
> "The investment is [$X] for [timeframe / deliverable]. Now [acknowledge that's significant]. But here's what most people don't realize: [why this is actually cheap compared to the alternative]."

Soften the price by anchoring it against:
- The cost of NOT solving the problem ("you're burning $4k a month in wasted ad spend")
- The cost of doing it themselves ("a senior copywriter would cost $200/hr and take 40 hours")
- The cost of the alternative ("a typical agency charges $30k/mo for this")

**9.3 The price-range tease (for high-ticket / custom)**
> "This is a custom solution, so we'll discuss exact pricing on the call. But just so you know the range: this is a [four-figure monthly / five-figure one-time] investment."

Used by views-to-clients for their hands-on YouTube DFY service. Doesn't hide the price, but defers the exact number to the qualifying call.

### Anti-patterns
- "We have several pricing tiers" → kills focus. Pitch ONE thing at ONE price.
- "Just $X" — minimizing language ("just", "only") makes the visitor suspicious
- Discounting heavily inside the VSL — discount is the weakest closer; save it for nothing

## Beat 10 — Risk Reversal (9:15-9:30)

### Job
Remove the last barrier. Make the guarantee specific, conditional, and confident enough that it almost feels absurd.

### Formulas

**10.1 The "you don't pay if X" conditional**
> "If you don't [specific outcome] in [specific timeframe], you don't pay. Period."

Used by views-to-clients and Unorthodox Digital. The conditional makes it real — generic "satisfaction guarantee" is weak; "if you don't book five qualified calls in 30 days, we refund every dollar" is strong.

**10.2 The "we keep working" guarantee**
> "If by [endpoint], the system isn't [outcome], we don't stop — we keep building on us until it does."

Used by Friday Labs AIOS example. Implies the speaker is so confident they'll work for free rather than let you fail.

**10.3 The "show up and learn" minimum-effort guarantee**
> "Show up to all [N] sessions, take notes, ask questions. If you don't think it was worth what you paid, email me after the last one and I'll refund every penny."

Used by Client Ascension. Makes the burden ridiculously low (just show up) and the upside high (refund + you keep what you learned).

### Anti-patterns
- "30-day money-back guarantee" with no conditional → feels like ecom default, doesn't increase conversion
- "Satisfaction guaranteed" → meaningless phrase
- Long fine-print descriptions of the guarantee — kills momentum. Two sentences max.

## Beat 11 — Scarcity + Two-Paths Close (9:30-10:00)

### Job
Frame the decision as a fork in the road. Establish real scarcity. Direct them to one specific next action.

### Formulas

**11.1 The two-paths close (the signature Client Ascension move)**
> "A year from now, you're going to be in one of two places. Place one: you're still [current pain state in specifics]. Place two: you're [outcome state in specifics]. The only difference is the decision you make right now."

The most reliable close pattern for cold-traffic VSLs. Forces a binary choice frame.

**11.2 The "every day you wait" cost frame**
> "Every single day you wait is another day of [specific cost — ad spend burning / leads slipping / time lost]. And with us, you can have a [outcome] in [short timeframe]."

Used by Unorthodox Digital. Converts inaction into active loss.

**11.3 The real scarcity close**
> "We open [N] application slots per quarter. When they're gone, the next cohort opens [later date]. Click below to apply."

Real scarcity (numbered cohort, real deadline) beats fake scarcity (urgency-without-deadline) every time. Use real.

**11.4 The "click below right now" direct CTA**
End every VSL with a specific physical action:
> "Click the button below this video. Fill out the application. We'll review and reply within 48 hours."

Three concrete steps. No abstract "if you're interested" — they ARE interested or they wouldn't have watched 10 minutes.

### Anti-patterns
- "Thanks for watching!" → never thank the visitor; CTA them
- "I hope this was helpful" → also never; you're not a YouTuber, you're closing
- "Hopefully I'll speak to you soon" → too soft; replace with a direct CTA
- Multiple CTAs at the end (book a call, follow my YouTube, join my newsletter) → ONE next action only

## Universal transitional phrases (use these to keep voice consistent)

These are the connective tissue that makes a VSL feel like a person talking instead of a script being read. Sprinkle throughout — every 30-60 seconds.

- "And look,..." (use before a softer admission or a vulnerability beat)
- "Now look,..." (use before a pivot or a redirect)
- "Here's the thing:..." (use before a key insight)
- "Now you might be thinking..." (use before objection handling)
- "Let me be clear about something..." (use before a disqualifier or honest beat)
- "Here's what's actually happening:" (use before a contrarian reframe)
- "Now I get it, ..." (use to validate a concern before answering it)
- "But here's what most people don't realize..." (use before mechanism or insight)

Overuse any of these and the VSL feels like a template. Use sparingly — one or two per beat.
